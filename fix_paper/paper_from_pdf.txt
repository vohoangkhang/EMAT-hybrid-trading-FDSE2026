Abstract —The unpredictable volatility of financial markets
makes forecasting and trading decision-making a significant
challenge. Simple time-series models often fail to capture the
transitions in market modes, variance risk, and the impact of
crowd psychology. This study proposes a groundbreaking
Multimodal Trading Agent framework that integrates Hidden
Markov Models (HMMs) to identify market conditions,
GARCH models to quantify volatility, and FinBERT to extract
sentiment from over 165,000 bilingual financial news articles.
This entire flow of features is integrated through a complex
CNN-GRU network architecture combined with an Enhanced
Multi-Aspect Attention Transformer (EMAT). Specifically, the
system maps forecasts to a discrete action space, optimizing
investment strategies. Empirical data were collected from 2019
to 2026 on three representative stocks: META, GOOGL, and
VNM. Through a backward logic mechanism and strict overlap
analysis, the empirical results demonstrate that the system not
only delivers impressive profit margins but also exhibits
excellent risk hedging capabilities, successfully avoiding severe
asset declines. This result confirms the immense potential of
integrating mathematical statistics, natural language
processing, and deep learning in modern financial systems.
Keywords—Financial time series forecasting, Market regime
detection, Volatility modeling, Sentiment analysis, Multi-action
stocktrading
I. I NTRODUCTION
In the early stages, financial forecasting research primarily
focused on enhancing traditional statistical models, such as
ARIMA and GARCH, to improve the accuracy of price and
volatility predictions[1], [2]. While SARIMA proved effective
for seasonal data, it exhibited significant limitations in long-
term forecasting [3] Although subsequent machine learning
methods, such as Random Forest and XGBoost, improved the
ability to learn non-linear relationships [4], [5], they still
struggled to fully exploit the long-term sequential structure of
financial time series, which is characterized by high noise and
non-stationarity [6].
The advent of deep learning, particularly Long Short-
Term Memory (LSTM) networks, marked a major turning
point due to its memory mechanism that enables learning
long-term dependencies, demonstrating superior
performance over traditional methods [7]. More recently, the
Transformer architecture, with its self-attention mechanism,
has further proven its advantage in processing complex
structured data and supporting efficient parallel training.
However, current pure Transformer models still rely
predominantly on technical price data and do not fully reflect
market behavioral and psychological factors [8], [9], [10].
To address these limitations, integrating sentiment
analysis from news via large language models like FinBERT
has significantly improved forecasting accuracy, especially
during periods of high market volatility[11], [12]. Current
literature indicates a clear shift from single-model approaches
toward hybrid, multi-model frameworks. Nevertheless,
integrated systems capable of simultaneously addressing
core aspects—including time-series forecasting, market
regime identification, risk estimation, and crowd sentimentanalysis—within a unified decision-making system remain
limited. This is the research gap that the EMAT trading agent
framework aims to bridge to meet the complexities of modern
stock markets.
II. RELATED WORKS
The period 2023–2025 marks a strong convergence of
Deep Learning, Transformers, and Sentiment Analysis in
financial forecasting. Regression networks, particularly
LSTM, remain a crucial foundation, having proven superior
to traditional statistical models in capturing long-term
dependencies [7]. This performance is further enhanced
when integrated with FinBERT sentiment scores, which
significantly reduces error indices [13] and improves returns
through intraday sentiment mining [14].
Concurrently, the parallel processing capabilities and
self-attention mechanisms of the Transformer architecture
have opened a new era for financial forecasting. Studies
demonstrate its superior capability in identifying price trends
via CNN-Transformer hybrids [8], optimizing multi-asset
strategies [15], and maintaining resilience during extreme
market volatility through ensemble attention mechanisms
[16].
Despite advancements in LLM-based sentiment
extraction [17], handling the non-stationary nature of markets
and text data noise remains a critical challenge [18]. To
address this, the latest research trend is shifting toward
multimodal "trading agents" designed to directly make
actionable decisions. While recent works have explored
combinations like PPO reinforcement learning [19] or PSO
with market sentiment [20] , most existing works still focus
on single combinations. A clear gap remains in building a
comprehensive trading agency system capable of
simultaneously integrating hidden market conditions
(HMM), volatility risk (GARCH), and multi-faceted news
sentiment to make multi-action trading decisions. This is the
driving force and solid theoretical basis for proposing the
EMAT framework.
III. METHODS
Making trading decisions in the stock market is a highly
complex problem, simultaneously influenced by time series
dynamics, underlying market conditions, stochastic variance
risk, and crowd psychology impulses. Instead of approaching
the problem using the traditional binary (buy/sell) forecasting
approach, which has shown many limitations in practice, this
study redefines the problem as a multimodal trading agent
system. Specifically, the expanded action space is designed to
include seven advanced capital management commands:

The overall EMAT architecture is built on a hybrid
superstructure, allowing for the integration of quantitative
data streams (OHLCV series and 12 core technical indicators)
and qualitative data streams (thousands of bilingual financial
news articles) to map flexible and optimal trading decisions.

To ensure the integrity and reliability of input data, the
study established a rigorous mathematical preprocessing
procedure. The operating model is based on a fixed sliding
window size of 10 days. This parameter selection stems from
the empirical evidence showing that a 10-session cycle
(equivalent to 2 working weeks) is the ideal timeframe to
capture short-term momentum without being diluted by
overly distant macroeconomic disturbances. The
breakthrough in preprocessing is the "Weekend Backward
Shift" mechanism. In traditional financial sentiment analysis
models, information generated at the end of the week is often
ignored or mechanically added to the Monday opening
session, causing a delay in data reflection [21]. To overcome
this, our algorithm handles the disruption between the market
closing cycle (trading hours) and the 24/7 news flow by
automatically aggregating all the emotional momentum from
news generated on Saturday and Sunday, then shifting back
to the time of the previous Friday according to the time series
alignment logic [22]. This mechanism allows the system's
state matrix to fully absorb the information "compression" at
the last anchor point of the trading week, thereby accurately
explaining the price jumps (gaps up/down) as soon as the
Monday morning trading session opens. The entire testing
process was also rigorously executed in real data overlap
areas from 2020 to 2025 for META and GOOGL, and from
2019 to 2025 for VNM, eliminating the risk of future
information leakage (look-ahead bias).

time series always exhibit non-linearity, non-stationary
properties, and extremely high noise levels. Recently, the
Transformer deep learning architecture has demonstrated
superiority in modeling long-term dependencies compared to
traditional sequential regression networks [23]. To fully
exploit this power, our study inherits and extends the
Enhanced Multi-Aspect Attention Transformer (EMAT)
network – a foundational architecture recently proposed by
Chen et al. [24] for financial time series forecasting. The
original EMAT framework is designed with a Multi-Aspect
Attention Mechanism to simultaneously capture temporal
decay, trend dynamics, and volatility regimes. From this
foundation, our research develops EMAT into a multi-modal
agency system by parallel-extracting price aspects and
integrating them with macroeconomic data. The EMAT
block is sophisticatedly structured with 6 stacked
Transformer Encoder layers, where the input layer
incorporates a Time2Vec embedding mechanism to help the
model automatically learn the seasonality of financial data.
Each Encoder layer operates through an 8-head Attention
mechanism with a hidden dimension space of 256 units and
a feed-forward neural network of 512 units. Notably, the use
of the GeLU activation function instead of ReLU helps the
model converge more smoothly in negative value regions.

Besides price momentum signals, a core principle in
empirical financial theory is that markets are always moving
through different regimes, and the effectiveness of any
strategy depends entirely on the current regime. The Hidden
Markov Model (HMM) is the optimal probabilistic model
for decoding these latent state variables [25]. In this study,
the system loads a macro characteristic space including
logarithmic returns, volume volatility, and price range (High-
Low Spread) into the HMM model. Based on the probability
matrix of state transitions, the HMM classifies the market
into three distinct regimes: Bullish, Bearish, and Sideways.
The output of the HMM block does not directly participate
in price forecasting but serves as a contextual filter, helping
the system determine whether to apply a trend-following or
mean-reversion strategy.

At the same time, to closely manage the "volatility
clustering" phenomenon – a factor that directly leads to
account shocks, research integrates the GARCH system
(1,1). First introduced by Bollerslev [26], the Generalized
Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH)
model is a natural generalization of the ARCH process,
allowing the direct inclusion of past conditional variances
into the current variance equation. This flexible lag structure
creates an optimal statistical benchmark in forecasting the
conditional variance risk of the market [27]. The model
calculates volatility according to the equation:

In this system, the coefficient α 1measures the sensitivity
of the market to the latest shocks, while β 1represents the
sustainability of past fluctuations. Based on the forecast
variance σ2
t, the system classifies the trading environment
into three risk levels (Low, Medium, High Risk), creating an
absolute quantitative basis for triggering defensive orders
instead of relying on sentiment.
To digitize crowd psychology, news texts are mapped
into 768-dimensional embeddings via FinBERT[28].
FinBERT takes news texts and transforms them into semantic
embeddings in a 768-dimensional space. However, instead
of directly using this result, the system deploys a complex
Sentiment Hybrid block to filter information. Here, a 1D
convolutional neural network (CNN) with a kernel size of 3
and 256 filters is used to scan through the vector sequence.
The kernel size of 3 allows the CNN to accurately extract
three-word phrases (trigrams) carrying strong impulse
signals such as "market crash" or "profit surge". This feature
map is then passed through a bidirectional feedback network
(BiGRU) with 128 units per direction (total output 256,
Dropout rate 0.3) to connect the long sequence to the context,
ultimately forming a highly sensitive Weighted Sentiment
score vector.
The final stage of the framework integrates state
representation and decision-making through a unified fusion
process. Latent representations generated by EMAT, HMM,
GARCH, and the sentiment module are concatenated into a
unified multidimensional state matrix. A secondary hybrid
CNN–GRU block is then applied to capture cross-component
correlations and regime transition patterns before forwarding
the fused representation to a fully connected layer. A Softmax
head maps the resulting features into seven discrete trading
actions.
Action execution is regulated by a strict rule-based
constraint layer. Strong Buy signals are activated only when
four conditions jointly hold: EMAT identifies an upward
trend, HMM confirms a bullish regime, GARCH indicateslow volatility risk, and sentiment is positive. Intermediate
actions (Hold, Hold–High Risk) enable position maintenance
under controlled risk, while a dedicated Wait state serves as
a portfolio protection mechanism under conflicting
macroeconomic and technical signals. When HMM signals a
bearish regime combined with volatility exceeding
predefined thresholds, all buy actions are invalidated and
automatic Strong Sell and Exit actions are triggered for loss
mitigation.
IV. EXPERIMENTS AND DISCUSSION
A. DatasetandImplementationSetup
The empirical study utilizes OHLCV series from 2019 to
2025 for META, GOOGL, and VNM. This technical data is
synchronized with a customized corpus of 1.53 million
bilingual financial news items sourced from platforms like
Benzinga, Zacks, and Vietstock. News items are mapped to
768-dimensional embeddings via FinBERT. To specifically
enhance domestic market analysis, 6,563 Vietnamese articles
were annotated with 11 standardized sentiment attributes to
fine-tune the qualitative branch.
Fig. 6. Quantitative analysis and trading signals for META stocks
B. Aggregation&PreprocessingMechanism
To prevent signal dilution inherent in simple averaging,
the system employs a rigorous confidence-weighted mean
aggregation process. Raw news texts, constrained to 512
characters, are evaluated through the FinBERT-CNN-
BiGRU block. The algorithm filters out noise by strictly
retaining signals with a minimum confidence level of 60%.
The daily representative sentiment score is then computed by
averaging these high-confidence probabilities, and a
differential threshold of 0.20 is applied to assign the final
aggregated sentiment label for the trading session.

Specifically, for META, the RMSE error decreased
sharply by 37.2% (from 15.24 to 9.57), while the MAPE was

and eliminated the inherent lag of traditional LSTM networks.

deep feature extraction capabilities, entirely independent of
random chance.

EMAT's decision layer guarantees strict transparency (Figure
10). A Strong Buy (Buy++) is exclusively executed when
technical trends are upward, HMM confirms a bullish state,
GARCH volatility is low, and sentiment is positive;
conversely, bearish regimes with high volatility automatically
trigger Sell/Exit actions to mitigate losses.

VI. CONCLUSION
This study successfully proposes EMAT, a multimodal
trading agent that transcends the limitations of traditional
binary financial forecasting. By integrating Transformer-
based spatio-temporal extraction with HMM regime
detection, GARCH volatility risk estimation, and FinBERT-
driven sentiment analysis, the framework rigorously maps
complex, non-stationary market dynamics into a discrete 7-
action space. Empirical evaluations confirm that the system
achieves state-of-the-art predictive accuracy (MAPE 1.88%)
and highly profitable, explainable trading signals (win rates
up to 87.18%). Crucially, its rule-based decision layer ensures
transparent risk hedging, successfully protecting portfolios
from severe asset drawdowns. Future research will explore
integrating Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents to enable the
system to continuously optimize dynamic multi-asset
portfolios in real-time trading environments.
